
# GROWING THINGS

**An experimental audiovisual instrument where watering a field causes things to grow, change, and make sound.**

GROWING THINGS explores how simple, rule-based materials can produce complex structures and evolving musical compositions.

The player pours water onto a three-dimensional grid. Rather than placing objects or directly controlling sounds, they influence an autonomous system. Water moves through the environment, growth emerges from local conditions, and the resulting structures determine what is heard.

## How It Works

### Water

Water falls, flows across surfaces, finds lower ground, evaporates, and spills off the edges of the grid. Stable structures can naturally contain water, allowing pools to form without explicitly defining them.

### Growth

Growth is generated by rule-based atoms capable of reproducing vertically and horizontally. Water sustains this activity. Without sufficient moisture, structures stop growing and begin to sink. Watering them again interrupts the decline and allows growth to resume.

### Sound

Sound comes from the geometry of the structures. Height, mass, and exposed surface area influence four sound beds assigned to quadrants of the field.

The quadrants define musical space, not particular growth materials.

## First Milestone

Establish a playable cycle using water and one simple growing material:

1. Water flows naturally, drains from the grid, and evaporates.
2. Growth emerges in response to watering, without manually placing growth atoms.
3. Growing structures increase in height and spread horizontally.
4. Growth subsides and begins sinking when moisture is depleted.
5. Additional watering restarts growth from the existing structure.

The precise rules for how growth first emerges remain experimental.

## Development

GROWING THINGS begins with the codebase developed for **AUDIBLE FIELD**, reusing its three-dimensional atom simulation, rule engine, renderer, input system, and four-quadrant Web Audio mixer.

The initial focus is on establishing the growth simulation. Additional materials, interactions between structures, environmental conditions, and automatic field rotation are possibilities for later exploration.

### Run Locally

From the project root:

```bash
npm install --prefix web-visualizer
npm start --prefix web-visualizer
```

Open [http://127.0.0.1:8080](http://127.0.0.1:8080).

The copied application retains AUDIBLE FIELD functionality until the new simulation is implemented.
